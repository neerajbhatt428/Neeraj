Percentage calculator — six questions, one tool

This is a free percentage calculator that answers the six percentage questions people actually ask. What is 15% of 200? What percent is 30 of 200? If I know a part and a percent, what's the whole? How much did something increase or decrease? How far apart are two numbers? And what was the original value before a percent was applied?

Every panel updates as you type — there's no Calculate button and no submit. Your inputs are saved to your browser's local storage, so they come back when you return. Nothing is uploaded, no account is needed, and the calculator works offline after the first visit.

How to use it

  1. Pick the question type. Six tabs across the top correspond to six different percentage questions. Click the tab that matches what you're trying to work out.
  2. Enter your numbers. Type them into the input boxes. The result appears immediately — there is no Calculate button and no submit.
  3. Read the result and the formula. The result box shows the answer, the numbers you entered, and the formula used, so you can verify the calculation by hand if you want.
  4. Copy or share. Click Copy result to copy the number to your clipboard, or Share link to copy a URL that reopens the calculator with your exact inputs.
  5. Switch tabs freely. Your inputs in every panel are preserved — the calculator remembers all six values, not just the active one.

What the six panels do

  • X% of Y — "What is 15% of 200?" → 30. Use this for tips, tax, discounts, and any "find the amount" question.
  • X is what % of Y — "30 is what percent of 200?" → 15%. Use this for test scores, grade percentages, and proportion questions.
  • X is Y% of what — the reverse question. "30 is 15% of what number?" → 200. Use this when you know a part and its percent but not the whole.
  • Change A → B — percentage increase or decrease. "Price went from 80 to 100" → +25%. Use this for price changes, salary hikes, growth rates.
  • Difference A ↔ B — how far apart two values are, without designating a starting point. "Difference between 80 and 100" → 22.22%.
  • Reverse % — you know the final value and the percent applied. "125 after a 25% increase" → 100. Use this to find the original price before a discount.

The formulas, in one place

  • X% of Y = (X ÷ 100) × Y
  • X is what % of Y = (X ÷ Y) × 100
  • X is Y% of what = X ÷ (Y ÷ 100)
  • Change from A to B = ((B − A) ÷ A) × 100
  • Difference = |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100
  • Reverse = final ÷ (1 ± X ÷ 100)

Percentage change vs percentage difference

Percentage change measures movement from a specific starting value. If 80 changes to 100, the change is 25% — because 80 is the reference.

Percentage difference compares two values without designating either one as the starting point. The reference is the average of the two. The difference between 80 and 100 is 22.22% because 90 is the reference.

Use change when there's a clear before-and-after (price went from ₹80 to ₹100). Use difference when comparing two independent values (temperature in two cities).

Percentage points vs percent

If an interest rate goes from 5% to 7%, that's a 2 percentage point increase — but a 40% relative increase. These are different things and confusing them is one of the most common mistakes in finance and statistics.

  • Percentage points — the arithmetic difference between two percentages: 7 − 5 = 2.
  • Percent change — the relative change: (7 − 5) ÷ 5 = 0.4 = 40%.

When you read "rates rose 2%" in the news, check whether they mean 2 percentage points (5% → 7%) or 2% relative (5% → 5.1%). It's often ambiguous.

What this calculator can't do

Being honest about limits is more useful than pretending they don't exist. This calculator does not:

  • Compound multiple percentages at once. If you need to apply successive changes (10% off, then 5% tax, then 3% shipping), calculate each step in sequence. The final price isn't the sum of the percentages.
  • Work with fractions or mixed numbers directly. Enter decimal equivalents, or use a dedicated fraction calculator.
  • Do statistical regressions. Mean, median, standard deviation and correlation need a dedicated statistics tool.
  • Convert between percentages and ratios or odds. "1 in 5" is 20%, but working with odds ratios needs a specialised tool.
  • Handle negative results that aren't meaningful. The percentage difference formula uses absolute values because a negative difference isn't well-defined for two unordered values.
  • Save calculation history. Only your last inputs in each panel are saved, not a history of past calculations.

Troubleshooting

  • Result shows "—". One of the inputs is empty or not a number. Check both fields.
  • Result shows "undefined". You've tried to divide by zero. In the "X is what % of Y" panel, Y can't be zero. In the "Change" panel, A can't be zero.
  • Percentage change looks wrong. Remember that going from 100 to 80 is −20%, but going from 80 to 100 is +25%. Percentage change is asymmetric because the denominators differ.
  • Reverse percentage gives an unexpected number. Check that you picked the right direction (increase vs decrease). A 25% increase and a 25% decrease are not inverses: if 100 increases to 125, dividing 125 by 0.75 gives 166.67, not 100.
  • Inputs disappeared. Your inputs are stored in browser local storage. Private browsing or clearing browser data will reset them.
  • Share link doesn't reproduce my inputs. Make sure you clicked Share link after typing the final value — the URL captures the values at the moment you click.

Common questions

How do I calculate X% of Y?

Multiply Y by X and divide by 100. For example, 15% of 200 is 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Divide the increase by the original value, then multiply by 100. Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

Divide the decrease by the original value, then multiply by 100 and add a minus sign. Going from 100 to 80 is a −20% decrease.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?

Change uses a specific starting value as the reference. Difference compares two values without designating a start — it uses the average as the reference.

How do I calculate reverse percentage?

If a value was increased by X% to reach a final number, divide the final by 1 + (X ÷ 100). If decreased, divide by 1 − (X ÷ 100).

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A change from 5% to 7% is a 2 percentage point increase, but a 40% relative increase. Percentage points measure the arithmetic difference; percent measures the relative change.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second, then multiply by 100. For example, 30 out of 200 is 15%.

How do I find the original number before a percentage was applied?

Use the Reverse percentage panel. If a value increased by 25% to reach 125, the original was 125 ÷ 1.25 = 100.

Are my inputs saved?

Can I share a specific calculation?

How do I calculate a discount?

Use the X% of Y panel to find the discount amount. For a ₹1,500 item at 30% off: 30% of 1500 = ₹450 off, so you pay ₹1,050. To reverse a discount, use the Reverse panel: ₹1,050 ÷ 0.70 = ₹1,500.

How do I calculate a tip?

Use the X% of Y panel. For a 15% tip on a ₹1,200 bill, enter 15 and 1200 to get ₹180.

How do I calculate test scores as percentages?

Use the X is what % of Y panel. If you scored 42 out of 50, enter 42 and 50 to get 84%.
