Scientific calculator built for engineering and science work

This is a free online scientific calculator that handles the mathematics engineering, physics, chemistry and statistics students actually need: trigonometry in three angle modes, logarithms and exponentials, powers and roots, factorials, combinations and permutations, scientific notation, and a memory register. It runs entirely in your browser — no account, no ads on this page, no upload, no limits on how many calculations you perform.

Unlike a physical scientific calculator, this one works on any device — phone, tablet, laptop, desktop — and its history is saved between sessions. Open the page on your phone during a lecture, continue the same calculations on your laptop in the lab, and the previous session's answers are still there to reference.

How to use it

  1. Set the angle mode. Tap the DEG chip in the top-left of the display to cycle between degrees (DEG), radians (RAD) and gradians (GRAD). The mode you pick applies to every trig function.
  2. Type the expression. Tap the on-screen buttons, or use your keyboard. Numbers, operators, parentheses and function keys all work.
  3. See the live result. The answer updates as you type. Press Enter or = to commit it to history.
  4. Use second functions. Tap 2nd to reveal inverse trig (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹), hyperbolic functions, cube root, any-root, and combinatorics (nCr, nPr).
  5. Store values in memory. Use M+ to add to memory, MR to recall, MC to clear. The M indicator lights up whenever memory holds a value.
  6. Reuse from history. Click any past calculation in the History panel to load it back into the calculator.

What you can calculate

Basic arithmetic

Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, parentheses and exponents. The order of operations is respected: 2 + 3 × 4 returns 14, not 20. Implicit multiplication works — 2π is treated as 2 × π.

Trigonometry

sin, cos, tan plus inverse (asin, acos, atan) and hyperbolic (sinh, cosh, tanh) variants. Switch angle mode in the top-left. Examples: in DEG mode, sin(30) = 0.5. In RAD mode, cos(π) = −1.

Logarithms and exponentials

ln is the natural log (base e). log is base 10. Second function gives you ex and 10x for exponential work.

Powers and roots

x² squares, xʸ raises to any power, √ takes the square root. Second function adds ∛ (cube root), y√x (any root) and x³.

Factorials and combinatorics

x! computes factorials (extended by the Gamma function for non-integers). nCr gives combinations, nPr gives permutations. Example: 5 choose 2 → 5 nCr 2 = 10.

Memory register

M+ adds the current value to memory. MR recalls it. MC clears it. The 2nd layer reveals M− (subtract) and MS (store, overwriting). Memory survives page reloads.

Scientific notation

Press EXP to enter notation like 1.5E-3 (meaning 1.5 × 10⁻³ = 0.0015). Very large or very small results are automatically shown in scientific form.

Common uses for students

  • Verifying trig identities. Check that sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for any angle by computing both sides.
  • Physics homework. Use EXP for scientific notation and memory for physical constants like g = 9.81.
  • Circuit analysis. Compute impedances using trig and powers on complex-valued magnitudes.
  • Statistics. Calculate combinations for binomial coefficients, or evaluate probability formulas.
  • Chemistry. Compute molar masses, dilutions and pH using logarithms.
  • Engineering conversions. Change units like km/h to m/s with a single expression: 100 × 1000 ÷ 3600.
  • Signal processing. Work with sin and cos values in RAD mode for Fourier analysis.
  • Unit circle practice. Quickly check sin, cos and tan at common angles.

What this calculator can't do

Being honest about limits is more useful than pretending they don't exist. This calculator does not:

  • Graph functions. It evaluates expressions. For plotting, use Desmos or GeoGebra.
  • Do symbolic algebra. It returns numeric answers, not simplified expressions. For algebra, use Wolfram Alpha or a computer algebra system.
  • Handle complex numbers. All inputs and outputs are real numbers. Complex arithmetic needs a specialised tool.
  • Solve equations. You can evaluate f(x) for specific values but the calculator won't solve f(x) = 0 for you.
  • Work with matrices or vectors. This is a scalar calculator. For linear algebra, use a matrix calculator.
  • Perform statistical regressions. Mean and standard deviation can be computed manually, but regression and hypothesis testing need a dedicated statistics tool.

Troubleshooting

  • Wrong trig answers. You're probably in the wrong angle mode. Check the chip in the top-left of the display — DEG for degrees, RAD for radians, GRAD for gradians.
  • Result shows "—". The expression is incomplete or has an error. Check for missing parentheses or an operator with no second operand.
  • "Division by zero". One of your denominators evaluates to zero. Check the divisor.
  • "Result too large". The computation exceeded the floating-point limit. Try smaller inputs, or work in logarithmic space.
  • History disappeared. History is stored in your browser's local storage. Clearing browser data, using private browsing, or switching devices loses it.
  • Keyboard doesn't work. Click once on the calculator area to give it focus, then type.

Common questions

Is this scientific calculator free?

Yes. It is completely free, runs entirely in your browser, and requires no signup.

Does it support degrees and radians?

Yes. Tap the DEG button to cycle between DEG, RAD and GRAD modes. The current mode is always shown on the display.

Can I do engineering calculations with scientific notation?

Yes. Use the EXP button to enter scientific notation. For example, 1.5E-3 means 1.5 × 10⁻³ = 0.0015.

Does it store my calculation history?

Yes. The last 20 calculations are saved in your browser's local storage. Click any entry to reuse the expression.

Does it work on mobile phones?

Yes. The layout is designed for touch, with buttons sized for fingers. It works on iPhone, Android and tablets.

How do I type mathematical expressions?

Tap the buttons or use your keyboard. Supported keys include numbers, operators, parentheses, and Enter for equals.

How accurate are the results?

Results use JavaScript 64-bit floating-point numbers, accurate to about 15 significant digits — the same precision as most graphing calculators.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, the calculator works without an internet connection.

What is the 2nd button for?

It reveals alternate functions on the keypad: inverse trig, hyperbolic functions, higher powers, cube root, and combinatorial functions.

Why does sin(30) show 0.5 in DEG mode but not RAD mode?

Because in RAD mode, 30 is interpreted as 30 radians, not 30 degrees. Switch to DEG for angle-based work.

Can it handle complex numbers?

Not directly. This is a real-number calculator. For complex arithmetic, use a CAS tool like Wolfram Alpha or a symbolic math engine.

Can it graph functions?

No. This is a calculator for evaluating expressions, not a graphing tool. For plotting, use Desmos or GeoGebra.

Does it support fractions?

It evaluates fractions as decimal results. Use parentheses to group the numerator and denominator, for example (1+2)/(3+4) = 0.428571…

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs in your browser. No calculations are sent to any server. History and memory are stored only on your device.
