Find your exact age — free, in your browser

This tool calculates your exact age in years, months and days from any birth date. Unlike a simple subtraction of dates, it counts full calendar years, then remaining calendar months, then leftover days — the same way age is defined on government forms, medical records and legal documents.

Everything runs locally in your browser. Your birth date never leaves your device, no account is required, and the tool works offline after the first visit. That makes it safe for family history research, medical forms, visa applications and anything else where you'd rather not hand a birth date to a random website.

How to use the age calculator

  1. Enter your birth date. Type the day, month and year into the three fields. The calculation runs instantly — no Calculate button.
  2. Choose a reference date (optional). By default the tool uses today. Switch to the Custom date tab to see how old you were (or will be) on a specific date.
  3. Read the result. You get years, months and days at the top, plus totals in months, weeks, days, hours, minutes and seconds below.
  4. Copy or share. Copy summary puts the full stats in your clipboard as plain text. Share link generates a URL with your dates embedded.

How age is calculated

Age is not just a subtraction of days. It is counted on the calendar, in three steps:

  1. Count full years. A full year has elapsed when the same month and day arrive in the following calendar year.
  2. Count full months. After whole years, count how many additional full calendar months have elapsed. A full month is not a fixed number of days — it depends on the month.
  3. Count remaining days. Whatever is left over, expressed in days.

Because months vary between 28 and 31 days, and leap years add an extra day to February, this calendar-based counting gives a different answer than simply dividing total days by 365. The tool follows the standard convention that matches every government ID, medical record and legal document. If you have ever seen two online age calculators disagree, it is almost always because one of them used the "divide days by 365.25" shortcut instead of the calendar.

Leap years and February 29 birthdays

February 29 exists only in leap years, which occur every four years except when the year is divisible by 100 but not by 400. The years 2000 and 2024 are leap years. 1900 and 2100 are not.

For people born on February 29, the tool handles the missing day in non-leap years in two ways:

  • Age calculation uses the true elapsed calendar time. On March 1 in a non-leap year, a February 29 person is one day past their "half birthday" and follows the same rules as any other date.
  • Birthday countdown treats March 1 as the birthday in non-leap years. This is the same convention used by most jurisdictions for legal age thresholds. Some regions use February 28 instead — the tool follows the more common March 1 rule.

Custom reference dates

The Custom date tab lets you calculate age on a specific day, either in the past or the future. Some practical uses:

  • Retroactive forms. Fill out a form that asks "age as of January 1, 2024" even if it is filled out months later.
  • Milestone planning. See exactly how old you will be on a target date, such as the day you retire, graduate or turn 50.
  • Medical and legal contexts. Determine precise age on the date an event occurred.
  • Historical research. Calculate the age of a historical figure on the day of a battle, treaty or publication.
  • Astrology. Some astrological systems need age to the day on a specific transit date.

The reference date can be set to any date from 1 AD to 9999 AD. The tool uses the proleptic Gregorian calendar — the same calendar extended backward before its official adoption in 1582.

Chinese zodiac

The Chinese zodiac runs on a 12-year cycle, each year associated with an animal. The order is Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig. One common gotcha: the Chinese New Year falls in late January or early February, so a person born in January or early February belongs to the previous year's animal. This tool uses the birth year directly without adjusting for Chinese New Year, which is the standard convention used by most Western tools.

Your privacy is protected

Every calculation happens in your browser. Your birth date, your reference date and any results are processed entirely on your own device. Nothing is sent to any server, logged, or retained.

Your inputs are stored in your own browser's local storage so they come back when you refresh or revisit the page. Clicking Reset clears this saved data immediately. No account is required, and no tracking cookies are used beyond what your browser sends by default.

The only network requests the page makes are for the fonts and, if you use them, the AdSense scripts. Everything else — the entire calculator — runs offline.

What this calculator can't do

Being honest about limits is more useful than pretending they don't exist. This tool does not:

  • Account for time of day. Ages are calculated at midnight boundaries. If you were born at 11 PM and it is currently 9 AM, the tool shows your age as of today, not as of 9 AM.
  • Handle time zones across history. Historical dates before 1900 used local solar time rather than time zones, and this tool does not model that.
  • Use the Julian calendar. Dates before 1582 are converted using the proleptic Gregorian calendar, which is the standard modern convention. If your research uses the Julian calendar, you need a dedicated historical date tool.
  • Adjust for Chinese New Year. The Chinese zodiac is based purely on the birth year. Someone born in January belongs to the previous year's animal in the traditional system, but this tool does not make that adjustment.
  • Provide Western zodiac with astronomical precision. Sign boundaries here are the calendar-date approximations used by most astrology sources. The exact moment the Sun enters a sign shifts by hours per year, so cusp births may differ from a chart-based tool.
  • Predict age-dependent outcomes. This is a date arithmetic tool, not a health or actuarial calculator.

Troubleshooting

  • The result card doesn't appear. The date is invalid — check that the day is valid for the month (for example, April 31 does not exist), and that the year is between 1 and 9999.
  • Age shows as "—". The reference date is earlier than the birth date. Switch the reference to today or pick a later date.
  • Leap-year birthday shows the wrong date. In non-leap years the tool uses March 1 as the fallback. If you need February 28, subtract one from the reference date and check the result again.
  • Saved data is out of date. The tool remembers your last inputs. Click Reset to clear them.
  • Chinese zodiac looks wrong. If you were born in January or early February, you likely belong to the previous year's animal. The tool uses the year directly without adjusting for Chinese New Year.
  • Weekday shows for a historical date. The tool uses the proleptic Gregorian calendar, which is accurate back to 1 AD. Historical dates before 1582 may have been recorded in a different calendar in the source you are reading.
  • Nothing appears after typing. Refresh the page once. Everything should then work offline.

Common questions

How is age calculated exactly?

Age is calculated by counting full calendar years, then full calendar months, then the remaining days between your birth date and the reference date. Leap years and months of different lengths are handled automatically, which is why this differs from simply dividing total days by 365.

What range of years does the calculator support?

Any year from 1 AD to 9999 AD. Historical dates work with the proleptic Gregorian calendar, which is the same calendar extended backward before its official adoption in 1582.

Why does my age in months differ from other calculators?

Because months have different lengths. Some calculators divide total days by 30.44 (the average month length), which gives an approximation. This tool uses real calendar months, the same way age is counted on official documents.

How do you handle February 29 birthdays?

In leap years, February 29 is your birthday. In non-leap years, the tool treats March 1 as your birthday for the countdown. The age calculation uses the actual elapsed calendar time, so a person born on February 29, 2000 is 24 years old on March 1, 2024.

What is my Chinese zodiac sign?

Your Chinese zodiac animal is determined by your birth year. The 12-year cycle runs: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig. Note that the Chinese New Year falls in late January or early February, so people born in January or early February belong to the previous year's animal.

How is the Western zodiac sign determined?

The Western zodiac sign is based on your birth month and day, following the standard date ranges used in Western astrology. Aries runs from March 21 to April 19, Taurus from April 20 to May 20, and so on through Pisces.

Can I calculate my age on a future date?

Yes. Switch to the Custom date tab and pick a future date. The tool shows how old you will be on that day, along with the weekday and total days until then.

Is my birth date saved anywhere?

Your inputs are stored in your own browser's local storage so they come back when you refresh or revisit the page. Nothing is sent to any server, and clearing the field or clicking Reset removes the saved data immediately.

Can I share my age calculation with someone?

Yes. Click Share link to copy a URL containing your birth date and reference date. Anyone who opens that link sees the same calculation.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. It runs in every modern mobile browser and installs as a web app if you add it to your home screen.

Does the age calculator work offline?

Yes. After the page loads once, everything runs locally. You can disconnect from the internet and keep using it.

Why does the age in years, months, days differ from my birthday math?

Because calendars are not uniform. A month can be 28, 29, 30 or 31 days. The tool uses the standard convention that counts full months on the calendar, matching how government forms, medical records and legal documents compute age.

How accurate is the next birthday countdown?

The countdown uses calendar dates rounded to midnight. It is accurate to the day in every case, including leap-year birthdays. It does not account for the exact time of day you were born, which most calculators ignore.

What is the life stage classification?

The life stage is a friendly label based on your age in years: Infant (under 1), Toddler (1 to 2), Child (3 to 12), Teenager (13 to 19), Young adult (20 to 29), Adult (30 to 44), Middle-aged (45 to 59), Senior (60 to 79) and Elder (80 and over). The boundaries vary by region, but the tool uses this common pattern.

How is this different from Adobe or other online calculators?

For date arithmetic, nothing. The difference is privacy. Every calculation runs on your device, no data is uploaded, and the tool keeps working offline after the first visit. Most online age calculators log the date you submit.
