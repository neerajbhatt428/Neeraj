Split PDFs visually — no page numbers to type

This is a free PDF splitter that works by clicking. Every page of your PDF is rendered as a thumbnail, and between any two pages you'll see a scissors icon. Click it to place a cut. The pages between two cuts become a separate PDF file. That's the entire workflow — no text fields, no "from page X to page Y" syntax, no guessing.

It runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your PDF is never uploaded, never logged, never retained. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and keep splitting. Safe for contracts, medical records, tax documents, and anything covered by an NDA.

How to split a PDF

  1. Open the PDF. Drop a file onto the drop zone or click to browse. Preview thumbnails of every page render automatically.
  2. Place cut points. Click the scissors icon between any two pages to split there. A red vertical line marks the cut. Click the scissors again to remove that split.
  3. Deselect pages (optional). Click any page thumbnail to deselect it. Deselected pages are excluded from every output PDF, so you can drop pages while splitting.
  4. Review segments. The Segments panel below the viewer shows every resulting PDF, its page range, and whether it will be exported. Toggle any segment to include or discard it.
  5. Split. Click Split PDF. The tool creates each output PDF in your browser.
  6. Download. Download each PDF individually or click Download all as ZIP to receive everything in one archive.

Deselect pages to drop them

Clicking a page thumbnail toggles whether that page is included. This is useful when you want to split a PDF and simultaneously remove cover pages, blank pages or ads. Deselected pages are excluded from all output PDFs. If an entire segment is deselected, it's marked "Skipped" and won't produce a file.

To select or deselect everything at once, use the toolbar buttons above the viewer.

How the split works

The tool uses two open-source JavaScript libraries:

  • pdf.js (Mozilla) — renders each page as a thumbnail for the preview strip.
  • pdf-lib — reads the original PDF, extracts the pages you selected, and writes them into new PDF files.
  • JSZip — bundles all output files into a single ZIP archive for one-click download.

Pages are copied byte-for-byte from the original PDF into each output file. Fonts, images, vector graphics, links and metadata all carry through unchanged. There is no re-compression and no quality loss.

File size and page limits

There is no hard limit, but preview rendering and page extraction happen in browser memory. Practical guidance:

  • Under 100 pages — instant on any device.
  • 100–500 pages — a few seconds to render previews on desktop, slower on mobile.
  • 500–1500 pages — preview strip loads slowly; consider using Delete PDF Pages or Organize PDF for very large files.
  • Over 1500 pages — may exceed browser memory. Compress first or split in stages.

The page previews are rendered at low resolution (about 0.4× scale) to keep the strip responsive. Output PDFs are always full quality.

Common uses

  • Extract a chapter. Split before and after the chapter's pages to produce a standalone PDF.
  • Separate invoices. Split a combined monthly bundle at each new invoice header.
  • Burst slides. Turn a slide deck into individual single-page PDFs.
  • Remove pages while splitting. Deselect cover pages, blank pages or ad inserts before exporting.
  • Prepare for uploads. Many systems cap uploads at 5 or 10 pages — split to fit.
  • Isolate a signed page. Extract the signature page from a contract as a separate file.
  • Share one section. Send only pages 12–18 to someone without sending the full document.
  • Reorganise. Split into parts, then use Merge PDF to combine them in a new order.

Your privacy is protected

Every operation happens in your browser. Your PDF is read into memory, split, and written back to disk by your own device. No PDF is ever sent to any server, logged, or retained.

The only network requests the page makes are for three open-source libraries — pdf-lib, Mozilla's pdf.js, and jszip — all loaded from a public CDN when the page first opens. After that, the tool works entirely offline. You can disconnect from the internet and keep splitting.

This makes the tool safe for documents you'd never upload to a cloud service: contracts, medical records, tax returns, bank statements, passport scans, legal filings.

What this splitter can't do

Being honest about limits is more useful than pretending they don't exist. This splitter does not:

  • Merge PDFs. Use Merge PDF for that.
  • Reorder individual pages. Use Organize PDF to reorder, duplicate or insert pages.
  • Compress the output. Each output PDF is roughly the size of the pages it contains. Use Compress PDF afterwards.
  • Open password-protected PDFs. If a PDF requires a password to open, remove it first. PDFs with only print/edit restrictions usually split fine.
  • Rotate pages. Use Rotate PDF for that.
  • Add page numbers. Use Add Page Numbers for that.
  • Convert to images. Use PDF to Image for that.

Troubleshooting

  • "PDF library not loaded" — the CDN is blocked or you're offline before the first page load. Refresh the page once. After that, the tool works offline.
  • Preview thumbnails don't render — the PDF is very large, or one page has unusual content. Wait a few seconds. Previews render one at a time.
  • Split fails with "Could not read PDF" — the file is likely encrypted, corrupted, or in a non-standard PDF variant. Open it in a PDF reader first to confirm it's readable.
  • Split is very slow — the total page count is over a few hundred. Try splitting one segment at a time by clearing split points first.
  • Some pages are missing from the output — check that you didn't accidentally deselect those pages. Deselected pages are excluded from every output file.
  • Bookmarks point to the wrong pages — this happens when a segment's pages were renumbered in the original. Split smaller ranges for cleaner output.
  • The ZIP download didn't start — some browsers block ZIP downloads if the file is very large (over 2 GB). Download individual PDFs instead.

Common questions

How do I split a PDF visually?

Click between any two page thumbnails where you want to split. A scissors icon appears, a red vertical line marks the cut, and the pages between cuts become separate PDF files.

Can I remove a split point?

Yes. Click the scissors icon again to remove that split and rejoin the pages.

Is this PDF splitter free?

Yes. Completely free, no page limit, no watermarks, no account required.

Can I split into uneven parts?

Yes. Place scissors anywhere — each segment can be a different length.

Does splitting reduce quality?

No. Pages are copied byte-for-byte from the original. Zero quality loss, no re-compression.

Are my PDFs uploaded?

No. Everything happens in your browser. Your files never leave your device.

What is burst mode?

Split at every page — every page becomes its own single-page PDF.

Can I remove pages while splitting?

Yes. Click any page thumbnail to deselect it. Deselected pages are excluded from all output files.

What's the maximum PDF size?

No hard limit. PDFs over 500 pages render previews slowly on low-end devices, but splitting still works.

Can I download all results at once?

Yes. Click Download all as ZIP after splitting to receive every output PDF in one archive.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Tap page thumbnails to select or deselect, tap the scissors to split, and download results directly to your device.

Can I split a password-protected PDF?

Not if the PDF requires a password to open. If it only has print or edit restrictions, splitting usually works.

What happens to bookmarks and links?

Bookmarks and internal links inside a kept segment are preserved. Links pointing to dropped pages may break.

Can I split and then merge in a different order?

Yes. Use Split PDF to create parts, then use Merge PDF to combine them back in any order.
