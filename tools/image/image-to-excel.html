How to convert an image to Excel

  1. Upload the image. Drag a screenshot, photo or scan onto the box above. JPG, PNG, WebP and BMP all work.
  2. Pick the language the text is written in. English is the default. The model downloads once and caches in your browser.
  3. Click Convert to table. OCR extracts every word, then an algorithm groups them into rows and columns using their position on the page.
  4. Fix any mistakes. The detected grid is fully editable. Click any cell and type the correct value.
  5. Download as XLSX. The result opens in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc and Numbers.

What works best

  • Screenshots of tables — near-perfect accuracy. This is the ideal case.
  • Photos of printed spreadsheets — accurate with good lighting and a straight-on angle.
  • Scanned documents — works well if the scan is 300 DPI or higher and pages are straight.
  • Handwritten tables — poor accuracy. Tesseract is designed for printed text.
  • Rotated or perspective-skewed photos — the column detection struggles. Straighten the image first.

How the table detection works

Tesseract returns each word with its exact pixel position on the image. The tool then runs three passes:

  1. Row detection. Words whose vertical centers are within the height of a typical character are grouped into a row.
  2. Column detection. All word left-edges are clustered. Values that fall within a small horizontal distance are treated as the same column.
  3. Grid assembly. Each word lands in a row × column cell. Multiple words in the same cell are joined with a space.

This works best when the source has clear horizontal alignment — which is why screenshots convert far better than photos.

Improving accuracy

  • Crop tightly to just the table. Extra content confuses the OCR.
  • Increase contrast. A light-grey table on a white background often benefits from being boosted before upload.
  • Straighten the image. Even 5° of tilt breaks column detection.
  • Avoid compression artifacts. Save screenshots as PNG rather than low-quality JPG.
  • Split large tables. If the table has more than 30 columns, convert it in halves.

Your image stays private

OCR runs entirely in your browser using Tesseract.js, a WebAssembly port of the open-source Tesseract engine. Your image is decoded in memory, processed locally, and discarded when you close the tab. Nothing is uploaded to any server. This makes the tool safe for invoices, bank statements and any document you'd rather not send to a third party.

Common questions

Is this image to Excel converter free?

Yes. Completely free, no signup, no watermarks and no page limit.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. OCR runs entirely in your browser using Tesseract.js. Your image never leaves your device.

What image formats are supported?

JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP and most common image formats. Screenshots, photos of printed tables, and scans all work.

How accurate is the OCR?

Accuracy depends on image quality. Clear screenshots of tables typically convert with 95%+ accuracy. Photos need good lighting and focus. Handwritten text will not convert reliably.

Can I edit the result before downloading?

Yes. The detected table appears in an editable grid. Click any cell to fix mistakes, then add or remove rows and columns before exporting.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. The tool works in modern mobile browsers, but large images are slower on phones because your device does the work.

Can I convert multiple images at once?

Not currently. Convert one image at a time and combine the resulting spreadsheets in Excel or Google Sheets.
