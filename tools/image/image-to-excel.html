    How to convert an image to Excel

    1. Upload the images. Drop up to 20 files, or click to choose. JPG, PNG, WebP and BMP all work. Each becomes a separate sheet in the workbook.
    2. Pick the language the text is written in and optionally restrict the character set (useful for tables that are only numbers).
    3. Optional: preprocess. Toggle auto-enhance, grayscale or threshold for photos. The original is never modified.
    4. Convert. OCR extracts every word with its position, then an algorithm groups them into rows and columns using the visual layout.
    5. Review the grid overlay in the Image view. If the detected lines look wrong, adjust preprocessing and reconvert.
    6. Fix any mistakes. Click a cell, type, or paste from the clipboard. Tab and arrow keys move like Excel.
    7. Export. Multi-sheet XLSX, CSV, JSON or TSV. Files open in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice and Numbers.

    What works best

    • Screenshots of tables — near-perfect accuracy. This is the ideal case.
    • Photos of printed spreadsheets — accurate with good lighting and a straight-on angle. Try the Adaptive threshold preprocessing.
    • Scanned documents — works well at 300 DPI or higher.
    • Handwritten tables — poor accuracy. Tesseract is designed for printed text.
    • Rotated or perspective-skewed photos — column detection struggles. Straighten the image first.

    How the table detection works

    OCR returns each word with an exact pixel position. The tool then runs three passes:

    1. Row detection. Words whose vertical centers fall within about two-thirds of the average line height are grouped into the same row.
    2. Column detection. All word left-edges are clustered. Groups that fall within 1.6× the average line height are merged.
    3. Grid assembly. Each word lands in a row × column cell. Multiple words in the same cell are joined with a space.

    This works best when the source has clear horizontal alignment — which is why screenshots convert far better than photos.

    Preprocessing explained

    • Auto-enhance — sharpens contrast and cleans up noise. Good default.
    • Grayscale — removes color distraction. Often improves accuracy on colored tables.
    • Adaptive threshold — converts to pure black and white, using local averages to handle uneven lighting. Excellent for photos.
    • Invert — for light text on dark backgrounds.
    • Upscale 2× — boosts accuracy on low-resolution screenshots and small text.

    Your images stay private

    OCR runs entirely in your browser using Tesseract.js, a WebAssembly port of the open-source Tesseract engine. Your images are decoded in memory, processed locally, and discarded when you close the tab. Nothing is uploaded. This makes the tool safe for invoices, bank statements and any document you'd rather not send to a third party.

    Common questions

    Is this image to Excel converter free?

    Yes. Completely free, no signup, no watermarks and no page limit.

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. OCR runs entirely in your browser using Tesseract.js. Your images never leave your device.

    Can I convert multiple images at once?

    Yes. Drop up to 20 images and each becomes its own sheet in the exported workbook.

    How accurate is the OCR?

    Clear screenshots of tables typically convert with 95%+ accuracy. Photos need good lighting and focus. Handwritten text will not convert reliably. Preprocessing toggles can help on difficult images.

    Can I edit the result before downloading?

    Yes. The detected table appears in an editable grid. Click any cell to fix mistakes, add or remove rows and columns, then export.

    Does it work on mobile?

    Yes. The tool works in modern mobile browsers, but large batches are slower on phones because your device does the work.

    Which export format should I use?

    XLSX for a fully formatted workbook (one sheet per image), CSV for a single-sheet import, JSON for developers, TSV for pasting directly into Google Sheets.
